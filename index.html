BBC WEB · PERSONALISATION STRATEGY

The BBC that knows you, edited by people you trust.

Personalisation on BBC Web isn't a feed to optimise — it's a relationship built on transparency, editorial judgement and user control. This is what that looks like in practice.

Editorial prioritiesYour interests
Balanced — mostly what matters today, with room for what matters to you.
Concept 01 — The Editorial Compass. On BBC Web, users can see and move this balance themselves. It never goes to zero editorial.
01

Product vision

BBC Web should feel like being guided by a brilliant, well-informed friend who happens to be a senior editor — someone who knows your interests, respects your time, and never lets you miss what matters.

The objective is not engagement. It's trust, comprehension and control. Every personalised element should make a user feel more informed, never more captured. Where commercial platforms optimise for attention, BBC Web optimises for an informed public — and personalisation is only justified when it serves that mission better than a purely editorial page could.

02

Core experience principles

  • 1

    Editorial-first, personalisation-second

    Personalisation reorders and augments; it never replaces editorial curation of what matters most.

  • 2

    Explainable by design

    Every recommendation carries a visible "why," written in plain language, not algorithmic jargon.

  • 3

    Control is ambient, not buried

    Users can see and adjust personalisation signals wherever they encounter them — not only in a settings page.

  • 4

    Discovery is structural, not incidental

    Space for content outside a user's known interests is reserved on principle, not left to an algorithm's afterthought.

  • 5

    Continuity over novelty

    The system's first job after any piece of content is answering "what next?" — before it guesses "what else generally?"

  • 6

    Two publics, one system

    News users want breadth, perspective and serendipity. Sport users want depth, continuity and team relevance. One architecture, two moods.

  • 7

    No dark patterns

    No infinite scroll built to trap, no engagement-bait headlines, no manufactured urgency.

03

Information architecture

A layered model, not a single feed — so a user can always mentally locate why they're seeing something.

LAYER 0 · FIXED
Editorial SpineTop stories, breaking news, homepage lead — fully editor-controlled, same for every user, always visible.
LAYER 1
Contextual ContinuationImmediately after any story: follow-up reporting, explainers, opposing viewpoints, live updates.
LAYER 2 · BOUNDED
For YouClearly labelled, capped in size, always adjacent to — never replacing — editorial sections.
LAYER 3
Discovery ZoneAn editorially curated "beyond your interests" rail, informed by what you don't usually read.
LAYER 4
Topic / Team / Competition HubsPersistent, followable structures giving personalisation a stable, legible backbone.
04

Personalisation model

Signals used — all user-visible

  • Explicit: followed topics, teams, competitions
  • Implicit-but-legible: recent reads, watches, in-progress stories
  • Contextual: time of day, device, location, live events happening now
  • Editorial overrides: importance scores that can outrank any personal signal

Design rule

No signal is ever used unless a plain-English explanation exists, and the user can down-weight, mute or reset it within two taps.

Blend logic

A visible ratio — roughly 70% editorial priority, 20% your interests, 10% discovery — shown as an honest mental model, never as an exact hidden algorithm.

05

Discovery experience

"Something Different"

Editorially hand-picked, rotated daily, explicitly framed as outside your usual reading.

Serendipity slot

After every 3rd–4th personalised recommendation, one deliberately diverse pick, honestly labelled.

Topic Explorer

A visual map of BBC's full topic breadth, so users can browse structurally, not algorithmically.

Perspective pairing

For contentious stories, "see this from another angle" as a standing feature, not a one-off.

06

Trust features

"Why am I seeing this?"

On every personalised card, one tap reveals the real reason — "You follow Arsenal," "Trending near you."

Provenance indicators

Clear sourcing for aggregated or live content.

Distinct visual language

Editorial and personalised content are styled consistently differently, so the layer is always legible at a glance.

Your BBC dashboard

A transparency report showing what data is used, in human language, updated in real time.

07

Control features

Interest dials

"More," "less," "why," "mute this" — available inline on any recommended card, not just settings.

Pause & reset

One tap to see the standard homepage, and a full personalisation reset.

Granular following

Follow a team, a topic, a journalist, or a single story thread.

Data control

View and delete specific signals — "forget that I read about X."

08

Recommendation placements

  • End of article or video — "Continue your journey"
  • Homepage "For You" module, capped at 4–6 items
  • Section fronts — a small personalised inset within editorial layout
  • Search results — personalised ranking hints, editorially bounded
  • Live event pages — personalised team or player callouts
  • Opt-in push / notification digest, with controllable frequency
09

News-specific experience

Emphasis on breadth and perspective over relevance-matching.

  • Explainer ladder: headline → explainer → analysis → perspectives, after any breaking story
  • Topic-following with built-in balance — "UK Politics" surfaces a range of viewpoints, never an echo chamber
  • Local and national content blended by location, always framed editorially
  • Contentious stories are paired with an alternate perspective by default
10

Sport-specific experience

Emphasis on relevance and continuity.

  • Followed-team hub as home base — fixtures, live scores, analysis, deep archive
  • "Continue the story" for ongoing competitions and seasons — a serialised journey, not a one-off feed
  • Second-screen live experience — personalised live-text prioritisation for followed teams during matches
11

Accessibility considerations

Fully operable controls

Every personalisation control works by keyboard and screen reader, with clear labelling for "why am I seeing this."

Stable layout

No personalisation-driven layout shifts that break navigation for assistive technology users.

Plain language

No jargon like "algorithmic weighting" — explanations meet WCAG readability standards.

Never colour-only

Editorial vs. personalised is always marked with an icon and label, never colour alone.

13

User journeys

A News reader

  1. Opens BBC Web and sees the Editorial Spine's top story
  2. Reads a health story
  3. "Continue your journey" offers an explainer, an opposing viewpoint, and one "Something Different" pick
  4. Taps "why am I seeing this" on the explainer, sees the reason, and follows the topic

A Sport fan

  1. Opens BBC Sport and sees their followed team's live match banner
  2. Taps into live text commentary
  3. After the match, "Continue" offers post-match analysis, the season table, and the next fixture
  4. Sets a fixture alert
14

Risks

  • Filter bubble creep

    Even with safeguards, discovery modules can be quietly deprioritised if engagement metrics creep back in.

  • Complexity fatigue

    Too many control dials could overwhelm rather than empower.

  • Trust erosion

    Vague or templated "why am I seeing this" explanations undermine the entire premise.

  • Editorial resourcing

    Hand-curated discovery content needs ongoing human effort, competing with automation cost savings.

  • Perceived bias

    Personalisation touching political content risks accusations of institutional bias without rigorous balance logic.

15–16

MVP and future vision

MVP
  • Editorial Spine unchanged
  • One bounded "For You" module — homepage and article end
  • Templated "why am I seeing this" tag
  • Follow topics and teams (extends existing capability)
  • One daily editor-picked "Something Different" slot
  • Simple pause / reset toggle
FUTURE VISION
  • Full "Your BBC" transparency dashboard with granular data control
  • Dynamic discovery zones, cadence-adaptive without compromising editorial independence
  • Cross-format continuation journeys — article → podcast → live event → follow-up
  • AI-assisted, human-approved perspective-pairing at scale
  • A personalised, auditable "trust score" generated from public methodology
TEN DIRECTIONS

Ten radically different personalisation concepts

Each supports discovery, editorial judgement, trust, user control and natural continuation — for News, Sport, or both. None of them try to be TikTok, YouTube or Netflix.

NEEDS SUPPORTED

Control, trust, editorial balance, understanding why.

PROBLEMS SOLVED

Opaque algorithmic blending; user helplessness over personalisation intensity.

BIGGEST RISK

Can feel gimmicky or add UI complexity if not simplified well.

MVP

A static slider with three presets — editorial-heavy, balanced, personal-heavy.

FUTURE

A fully dynamic, per-section, remembered dial with live visual feedback as content shifts.

Why it feels distinctly BBC — no commercial platform gives users literal control over the editorial-versus-personal ratio. It makes public-service values tangible.
NEEDS SUPPORTED

Continue journeys, relevant content, trust, staying informed.

PROBLEMS SOLVED

Users losing track of evolving stories; poor "what happened before this" context.

BIGGEST RISK

Editorial overhead to maintain threads consistently.

MVP

Manually curated threads for the top five to ten ongoing stories.

FUTURE

Semi-automated thread detection with editorial approval, cross-linked across News and Sport.

Why it feels distinctly BBC — it prioritises comprehension over engagement-chasing, a fundamentally editorial structure rather than an algorithmic one.
NEEDS SUPPORTED

Discovery beyond interests, editorial guidance, control — tickets can be skipped or muted.

PROBLEMS SOLVED

Discovery modules being scrolled past and ignored.

BIGGEST RISK

Gamification can feel un-BBC if overdesigned.

MVP

A simple weekly "three stories chosen for you by editors" module.

FUTURE

Picks based on genuine topic distance from known interests, with a visual map of what's been explored.

Why it feels distinctly BBC — it treats discovery as a deliberate editorial gift, not an algorithmic accident.
NEEDS SUPPORTED

Range of viewpoints, trust, avoiding filter bubbles.

PROBLEMS SOLVED

Echo chambers forming around political or contentious topics.

BIGGEST RISK

False-balance risk on issues with clear factual answers — must be scoped to genuine debate, not settled science.

MVP

Manual pairing for major political stories.

FUTURE

A structured "perspective index" showing coverage breadth across the spectrum for any topic.

Why it feels distinctly BBC — it directly operationalises the BBC's impartiality mandate as a UX feature, not a policy statement.
NEEDS SUPPORTED

Trust, transparency, understanding recommendations.

PROBLEMS SOLVED

Users never noticing or using a hidden trust feature tucked away in a menu.

BIGGEST RISK

Visual clutter if the marking system isn't kept minimal.

MVP

A two-mark system — personalised versus editorial — with one tap for detail.

FUTURE

A full mark and icon taxonomy applied consistently across every content type and placement.

Why it feels distinctly BBC — transparency becomes part of the visual grammar of the page, not an optional extra.
NEEDS SUPPORTED

Transparency, control, trust.

PROBLEMS SOLVED

Users being surprised or confused by what appears on a given visit.

BIGGEST RISK

Can feel whimsical or inconsistent with the gravity of serious news days.

MVP

A small text summary at the top of the homepage.

FUTURE

A visual, interactive forecast users can adjust for the day ahead.

Why it feels distinctly BBC — a warm, human framing device instead of an algorithmic dashboard.
NEEDS SUPPORTED

Relevant content, recent context, avoiding overly broad personalisation.

PROBLEMS SOLVED

Topic-following that's too coarse to feel truly relevant.

BIGGEST RISK

Requires much finer editorial tagging infrastructure.

MVP

A pilot with ten to twenty hand-tagged threads within News.

FUTURE

User-suggested thread creation, editorially moderated before publishing.

Why it feels distinctly BBC — precision in service of understanding, not precision in service of targeting.
NEEDS SUPPORTED

Trust, editorial guidance, discovery.

PROBLEMS SOLVED

Algorithmic voice feeling cold or untrustworthy.

BIGGEST RISK

Editorial resourcing to write these regularly; risk of feeling performative if overused.

MVP

A weekly editor's note on the homepage discovery module.

FUTURE

Rotating editorial voices per section, building visible human accountability into personalisation.

Why it feels distinctly BBC — it makes editorial trust personal and human rather than corporate — no other platform does this.
NEEDS SUPPORTED

Continuity, relevance, deeper journeys.

PROBLEMS SOLVED

Sport fans currently piecing together fragmented content across separate match reports.

BIGGEST RISK

Complexity of maintaining rich structured data per team and competition.

MVP

A basic hub combining fixtures and recent articles for followed teams.

FUTURE

A rich narrative layer — rivalries, player arcs, historic context — auto-updated each season.

Why it feels distinctly BBC — a companion built for a whole season, not a feed refreshed for a single session.
NEEDS SUPPORTED

Trust, control, transparency, understanding.

PROBLEMS SOLVED

Personalisation that feels invisible and untraceable.

BIGGEST RISK

Can feel surveillance-adjacent if framed wrong — needs a careful, reassuring tone.

MVP

A simple weekly summary page.

FUTURE

A full interactive ledger with edit and delete controls per entry, tied into the Your BBC dashboard.

Why it feels distinctly BBC — radical transparency as the trust mechanism — the opposite of opaque commercial personalisation.
